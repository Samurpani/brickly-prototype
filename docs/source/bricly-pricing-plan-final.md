# Bricly Pricing Plan (Final)

The canonical pricing reference. Recurring per-development engine, size-banded, product-priced. EUR and USD cards. Supersedes all earlier pricing documents.

---

## 1. The model in one paragraph

Bricly charges for the product, never a percentage of the developer's sales. The price has two parts: a recurring fee per development under management, banded by development size, and a one-time activation fee per development, priced by how much production the developer needs. Onboarding, managed campaigns (Bricly Performance), and usage overage are separate lines. Only the recurring fee counts as ARR. Pricing is private and quoted in a demo against the developer's own projects.

---

## 2. Recurring platform fee

**Per development under management, per year. Banded by size.**

The recurring fee covers the CRM, the personalisation engine, the AI sales agent, the usage allowance, and unlimited users. It runs the sales operation for a development from launch through to the last unit sold, and it is paid every year the development is under management.

| Development size | EUR / year | USD / year |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 50 units | €20,000 | $25,000 |
| 51 to 150 units | €35,000 | $45,000 |
| 151 to 300 units | €60,000 | $75,000 |
| 300+ units | Custom | Custom ($100K+) |

Why size bands: a 250-unit development consumes the product many times harder than a 10-unit one. More inventory, more buyer packs, more agent load, a larger allowance. The band prices product capacity, not project value, which keeps Bricly product-priced rather than commission-priced. Unit count is objective and verifiable at signing.

The floor is €20,000 / $25,000. Small boutique developments pay the floor. We do not price below it.

Known accepted leak: premium low-unit-count stock (for example 40 luxury units at €2M each) underprices slightly on a unit-count band. Accepted deliberately to keep pricing product-pure.

**Renewal path:** rates step up at renewal once case studies exist. Documented target €25K to €30K on the base band. Private pricing allows repricing without public friction.

### Portfolio view

Tiers are descriptive labels for portfolio size, not separate products. A developer's recurring total is the sum of their developments' bands.

| Label | Developments | Indicative recurring (standard-size devs) |
|---|---|---|
| Single | 1 | €20,000 / $25,000 |
| Foundation | 5 | €100,000 / $125,000 |
| Studio | 10 | €200,000 / $250,000 |
| Scale | 18 | €360,000 / $450,000 |
| Enterprise | 19+ | Custom |

## 3. Activation fee (one-time, per development)

The one-time fee to set a development up in Bricly and get it ready to sell. The grade depends on what the developer already has. A launcher always pays the launch rate; production is never available at a load price.

| Type | EUR | USD | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | €15,000 | $20,000 | New development with nothing built. Bricly produces the brand, website, renders, brochure, and ad creative, and builds the constraint model. |
| Load + personalisation | €7,000 | $10,000 | Existing development with brand and renders. Bricly builds the constraint model from their CAD so renders personalise per buyer, and migrates inventory. |
| Load, CRM-only | €4,000 | $5,000 | Existing development, CRM only. Inventory and static-asset migration. No constraint model, no personalisation. Upgradeable when CAD is provided. |

Activation runs at roughly 70% gross margin in the concierge era and rises toward software margin as Studio automates production.

| Output block (usage overage) | €1,800 / 10,000 credits | $2,500 / 10,000 credits | Auto-replenishing. Rarely triggers. |

## 5. Usage credits

Every output and AI action consumes an internal credit pegged to compute cost (~€0.10/credit blended, set high to absorb multi-pass renders and agent load). The developer never sees raw credits, only a plain-language allowance.

| Output | Credits |
|---|---|
| 1 render | 10 |
| 1 personalised buyer pack | 15 |
| 1 brochure | 25 |
| 1 brand identity package | 75 |
| 1 development microsite | 100 |
| AI agent activity | ~1 per task |

**Consumption drivers:** launch generation ~1,000 credits per development; buyer customisation ~150 credits per unit; AI assistant and agent ~7,500 credits per active user per year (~€750/user/year, the dominant variable cost).

**Allowances scale with the size band**, sized at expected consumption plus 25% headroom so normal accounts never see overage. Overage is a cost ceiling, not a revenue line.

## 6. Bricly Performance and the success fee

Performance is the optional done-for-you campaign arm. It is an acquisition channel first and a revenue line second: land a developer on a retainer, prove the pipeline, convert them to the platform. Services margin ~40%. Hard-capped at 25% of total company revenue.

**The success fee is a flat per-close marketing bonus, not a percentage of sale price.** €1,500 to €3,000 (or $2,000 to $4,000) per closed unit attributed to a Performance-run campaign, banded by unit price. Rules:

- Lives only inside Performance. Never attaches to the platform subscription.
- Attribution = first-touch source in the Bricly CRM from a campaign Performance runs, visible to the client in their own pipeline. Reconciled quarterly.
- Booked as contingent services revenue. Never in ARR. Counts against the 25% cap.
- Percentage-of-sale compensation is explicitly rejected: it contradicts the anti-commission pitch, invites attribution disputes, and triggers estate-agency licensing exposure in Malta, across the EU, and in most US states. Legal review per market before any success-fee contract ships.

## 7. Positioning rules

- **Product-priced, never commission-priced.** Agency commission (4 to 10% of GDV in Europe, 5 to 6% standard on US new construction) is the anchor in the demo, never the formula in the contract. On a €12M development, 5% commission is €600K; the Bricly fee is a small fraction of it. That comparison is shown in the room, not written into pricing.
- **Private pricing ("Based Only").** No public price list. A developer sees a number in a demo, against their own project, after the value is anchored. This allows high anchoring, per-developer pricing, and frictionless repricing. Modelled on Legora (no public pricing, ~$30K entry, $1M to $100M ARR in ~18 months).
- **One market, one card.** EUR card for Europe, USD card for the US. Never shown together. If asked: pricing is quoted per market against local commission benchmarks.
- **ARR discipline.** Only the recurring per-development fee is ARR. Activation, onboarding, Performance retainers, and success bonuses are stated separately. The trap is becoming an agency; the recurring line is what earns a software multiple.
- **Premium service is part of the price.** Every developer gets a dedicated account rep. High-ticket pricing funds high-touch service for a premium ICP.

## 8. Key economics (reference)

At the EUR card, standard mix: blended recurring ACV ~€166K; recurring gross margin ~92.5%; ~30 customers to €5M recurring ARR (vs ~985 on the original low-ticket model); CAC €20K; payback ~1.6 months; LTV:CAC ~53x. USD card runs ~10 to 15% above FX conversion as deliberate US premium; blended recurring ACV ~$190K to $200K.

Churn is modelled as a renewal event on annual contracts (target 85 to 90% gross renewal), not monthly SaaS churn.

Expansion is structural: the bill grows as a developer loads more developments and as developments move up size bands. NRR above 100% without new logos.
